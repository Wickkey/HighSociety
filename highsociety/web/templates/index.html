New to High Society? How to play

Goal: End the game with the most points — but there's a catch. When the game ends, whoever has the least money left is eliminated from winning entirely. If two or more players are tied for the least money, none of them are eliminated — everyone stays in contention. Only then, among whoever remains, does the highest score win.

Each round, one card is auctioned. There are two opposite auction types:

Normal auction — Paintings, the Prestige Card
Players take turns raising the bid. Passing means you're out. The last player standing wins the card and pays their bid.
Disgrace auction — Faux Pas, Passe, Scandale
Same bidding, but the first player to pass gets stuck with the card and gets their money back. Everyone else forfeits their raised money.

Money cards are locked in once committed to a bid — you can add more on a later turn to raise, but can't take any back until the auction resolves (unspent bids are returned if you don't win).

How is the winner decided?

First, anyone with the least money left is eliminated from winning entirely. If two or more players are tied for the least money, none of them are eliminated — everyone stays in contention. Among whoever remains, the highest total points wins. That total is calculated in two steps, in order: first add up the value of every painting you hold plus a Passe's −5 (if you hold one); only then does any Prestige Card (×2) or Scandale (½×) you hold multiply that whole total. Ties for the highest score are possible and simply win together.
